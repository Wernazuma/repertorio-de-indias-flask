Where ARCA comes from

The idea of ARCA was born from discussions between the project's leads on how to expand the work done by HGIS de las Indias. That original project centered on the geography of Spanish America in the Bourbon period (1701–1808), also linking demographic, socio-economic, and other data to its spatial entities. But the close of the funding period, together with the project's limited scope and its own “gravitational pull”, prompted a reboot with a wider approach: ARCA.

ARCA expands the theoretical scope considerably:

  • From the 18th century to the 21st — and back to the 16th, or even beyond.
  • From Spanish America to Portuguese, French, Anglo-American, and Indigenous spheres.
  • From geographic entities and concepts to entities and concepts of other domains.
  • From a limited set of individual contributions to an “open-world datascape” interconnected through the frame — a pool of data that fills up over time, weaving an ever denser net of interrelated information.

Paths of expansion

The first step toward ARCA is the option to contribute, transform, and match historical data to HGIS de las Indias — and to provide curated knowledge on the existing geographic entities. Bring your data →

With more funding — ideally as part of a thematic research project — the geographic core, the gazetteer of places and territorial units, can be extended to other areas and periods, letting us connect more and more datasets. If you are planning a project that would benefit from an existing shared framework, join forces: contact us to help build it as part of your project.
