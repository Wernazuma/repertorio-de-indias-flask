Atlas · History

A digital infrastructure for Bourbon Spanish America

HGIS de las Indias was conceived in 2014 to address a critical gap in the field of colonial Spanish America — and many others: the lack of historical geodata and shapefiles of historical territorial units, and more generally of a spatiotemporal digital infrastructure.

While most historical data inherently possess a geographic component, this context is frequently lost in traditional tabular or textual formats, leaving researchers without a clear “mental map” of the past.

The project was primarily funded by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF) and developed at the University of Graz by principal investigator Werner Stangl from 2014 to 2017. Originally set out only to reconstruct the territories of the last colonial decades, it soon managed to integrate the entire 18th century. The reconstruction and gazetteer of places likewise came to rival the territorial reconstruction in importance.

Over the years, further data — mail routes, colonial institutions, demography — was added to the HGIS de las Indias core.

Collaborations and expansion

As the project evolved, it shifted from a narrow research tool to a broad domain infrastructure through a series of key international collaborations.

Atlas Digital da América Lusa

Tiago Gil, University of Brasília. A pivotal partnership that allowed the integration of comparable data from Portuguese America into the HGIS WebGIS.

lhs.unb.br ↗

Dorothy Tanck de Estrada

Thanks to generous access to her database, HGIS de las Indias could integrate 4,000 indigenous settlements in Mexico from her Atlas ilustrado de los pueblos de indios in a single stroke — instrumental in verifying the inclusion or exclusion of towns within specific jurisdictions over time.

LatAmGazetteer

Led by Gimena del Río Riande and Ben Brumfield, this initiative cooperated with HGIS de las Indias to extract and match data from the historical dictionary of Antonio de Alcedo, which had already partly been used during the HGIS research.

pelagios.org ↗

World Historical Gazetteer

Through contact with its then technical lead Karl Grossner, the WHG became a primary partner for making HGIS data part of a global Linked Open Data ecosystem. Colonial Latin America — centred on HGIS de las Indias data — was selected as the first “domain” within this global infrastructure.

whgazetteer.org ↗

Pelagios Network & Recogito

Through collaboration with Rainer Simon, the project's data was integrated into the Recogito environment, allowing researchers to tag geographical references in historical texts and link them directly to the HGIS gazetteer.

pelagios.org ↗

DECM — Digging into Early Colonial Mexico

A research partnership with Patricia Murrieta-Flores at Lancaster University, seeking to link 18th-century infrastructure with 16th-century data extracted via machine learning from sources such as the relaciones geográficas.

lancaster.ac.uk ↗

TopUrbi

Following the conclusion of its initial funding phase, Werner Stangl docked at TopUrbi (Topographie de l'urbanisation impériale hispanique), funded by the French ANR, based at EHESS-CRH, with PI Jean-Paul Zúñiga and technical lead Carmen Brando. TopUrbi focuses on a digital edition and geocoding of Alcedo's dictionary to reconstruct the urban landscape at the end of the colonial period. Inspired by the concept of géographies croisées (entangled geographies), it confronts Alcedo's textual data with census and cartographic material to discuss historical biases and angles.

anr.fr ↗

ARCA and Yale

Today the project is moving — and slowly dissolving — into ARCA (Archive and Resource Center for the Americas), an initiative developed between José Antonio Espín-Sánchez and Werner Stangl, designed to unite disparate datasets into a shared framework and ensure the long-term sustainability and interoperability of colonial research. It is currently hosted at Yale University thanks to the support of Ron Borzekowski of DISSC, Jennifer Marlon of the Yale Center for Geospatial Solutions, and their staff.
